## Purpose and safe-use boundary

This note translates public, official descriptions of the Istana landscape into visual-form guidance for source-side vegetation work. It is intended for silhouette, material, colour, and density review only.

It is not a current botanical inventory, a survey, or an as-built record. It contains no precise tree coordinates, security or access layout, hidden routes, sensor implications, or inferred placement. Public source pages may contain maps or more detailed location language; those details are deliberately not reproduced here. Do not use this note to position an individual plant, infer a current specimen count, or claim that a historically recorded specimen remains present or unchanged.

All official photographs remain reference-only. This file embeds no source imagery and grants no reproduction rights. Check the publisher's current terms and obtain any required permission before copying official media [S9].

Confidence labels apply only to the cited public claim:

- **HIGH**: a current official page or an official record directly describes the Istana-related feature.
- **MEDIUM**: an official dated event or species profile supports the visual cue, but current on-site condition or use is not established.
- **LOW**: a project-authored proxy interpretation used for variety; it must not be presented as a site or species fact.

## Publicly supported landscape character

The President's Office describes the grounds as a verdant sanctuary and botanical haven with several kinds of managed landscape, including lawns, a fairway, a Japanese garden, a pond setting, and a formal terrace. The same page's public image descriptions repeatedly show expansive maintained grass, mature shade trees, scattered trees across open lawn, and denser tree framing at the edges [S1]. This supports a readable contrast between open turf and mature canopy masses. It does not establish dimensions, precise adjacency, or a complete set of vegetation.

The President's Office also states that NParks officers care for trees and turf across the grounds, manage fruit trees and a nursery that cultivates flowering plants and ornamental palms, and care for a wider diversity that includes endangered palm species [S2]. This supports maintained turf, broad tree diversity, and species-neutral palm forms. It does not identify the palm species, demonstrate where nursery plants are displayed outdoors, or provide a current census.

NHB's Roots article identifies Rain Trees at the Istana entrance and explicitly calls their crowns umbrella-shaped [S3]. An NParks Heritage Tree record independently associates a Rain Tree (*Samanea saman*) with the Istana grounds and describes a broad umbrella crown, dark rough bark, fine compound foliage, and pinkish or whitish flower clusters [S4]. These records support the umbrella family as the strongest site-linked tree silhouette. No row, position, or count is carried into this reference.

NHB describes the Istana grounds as well-manicured and a sprawling green lung [S8]. Together with the official grounds and staff pages, this supports an intentionally maintained, humid-tropical estate character rather than unmanaged scrub.

## Seasonal and colour cues

Singapore has an equatorial climate with high humidity, abundant rainfall, and broadly uniform temperatures through the year. The Meteorological Service describes two monsoon periods and inter-monsoon periods, while noting that Singapore does not have a distinct wet and dry season [S7]. The following art direction is therefore an explicit inference, not an Istana horticultural measurement:

- Use a year-round green baseline across canopy and turf; do not apply a synchronized temperate autumn palette or estate-wide winter dormancy.
- Retain individual variation. NParks describes Rain Tree as partially deciduous under local conditions, so limited thinning on individual crowns is plausible, but a synchronized leaf-off pass is not supported [S4a].
- Pink-white Rain Tree flower clusters may appear as a restrained, optional close-detail cue. Do not turn them into a dominant estate-wide colour field [S4][S4a].
- A light-purple or pale-lilac Binjai bloom may be an optional April-June variant only. It must not be presented as a statement that a tree is blooming now [S5].
- The nursery's flowering plants and ornamental palms support controlled accent colour and palm variety as categories, but not a particular outdoor bed, display, or season [S2].

## Concrete non-geographic modeling targets

The values below are deterministic project acceptance targets, not measurements recovered from the grounds. They apply in object space or image space and carry no world position.

1. **Silhouette families:** ship at least three visibly distinct variants each for `umbrella`, `dome`, and `high-fork`, and at least two each for `columnar` and `palm`. A mirror or yaw-only duplicate does not count as a new silhouette.
2. **Umbrella readability:** at the asset's neutral orthographic review angle, target crown-width-to-crown-height ratio `>= 1.35`, at least three non-uniform outer lobes, and a laterally spreading primary branch read. This is a proxy target, not a Rain Tree measurement.
3. **Dome readability:** target crown-width-to-crown-height ratio `0.90-1.35`; preserve at least two asymmetric edge breaks so the result does not read as a sphere. Use large, non-uniform leaf clumps when invoking the sourced Binjai-like cue.
4. **High-fork readability:** target visible clear bole at `35-55%` of total proxy height before the dominant fork, with unequal branch angles and an off-centre crown mass. Treat this family as generic.
5. **Columnar readability:** target total-height-to-crown-width ratio `>= 2.0`. A Binjai-influenced version must transition into a dense dome; a Sempilor-influenced version must use fine conifer-like sprays rather than broadleaf cards.
6. **Palm readability:** show a legible stem-to-frond transition and at least `7` separated crown directions in the close audit. Avoid broadleaf-tree forks and keep asset/display names species-neutral.
7. **Small-scale recognition:** at both `64 px` and `256 px` rendered proxy height, a reviewer must be able to distinguish umbrella, dome, high-fork, columnar, and palm silhouettes without reading labels.
8. **Turf continuity:** in a fixed-camera source-side audit, turf must read as one maintained macro-surface without abrupt density rings at the nominated near/mid/far samples. Use at least three deterministic albedo/roughness microvariants and keep bare-patch breakup localized rather than periodic.
9. **Colour restraint:** keep the baseline canopy/turf family green. Allow only optional localized pink-white Rain Tree and light-purple Binjai flowering variants from the sourced cues above; the default audit must not imply a synchronized bloom.
10. **Determinism:** fix variant selection, transform jitter, colour choice, camera, and lighting seed in the audit. Repeating the same command against unchanged inputs must produce byte-identical source-side audit metadata and imagery.

These targets intentionally avoid height in metres, planting density per hectare, sightline placement, route adjacency, coordinates, and tree-by-tree matching.

## Acceptance checklist

- Every species label is traceable to an explicit official source in the ledger below.
- Every historical record retains its date and is not silently upgraded to a current-inventory claim.
- Proxy-family judgments are described as resemblance, not identification.
- Palm assets remain species-neutral unless separately sourced.
- Turf remains a maintained tropical visual layer without invented species or maintenance specifications.
- No map, coordinate, access restriction detail, security layout, hidden path, or sensor implication is added.
- No official photograph or other source image is embedded or copied.
- A deterministic, non-networked contract test validates this document's schema, source roster, safe-use markers, and proxy roster.
